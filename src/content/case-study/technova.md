overview:
  title: "Start With a Spring and a Prediction"
  content: |
    In this illustrative lesson, the teacher asks what might happen to a spring when its load changes. Learners make a prediction, then vary the load while keeping the spring fixed. They record the simulated extension and talk through the pattern with the teacher’s learning objective in mind.

    The scenario explores how the VLABS design could support that sequence. It is not an account of a deployed classroom or a measured result; pilot content, simulation accuracy and available tools need agreement before use.
challenges:
  title: "Ask How They Reached That Answer"
  content: |
    A learner may correctly predict that the spring will stretch further and still be unsure why. A classmate may understand the relationship but change both the spring and the load. Hearing their explanations gives the teacher something to talk through: the idea itself, the way they tested it, or both.

    Where equipment or time is limited, virtual practice can support preparation and repetition. It complements physical practical work, supervision and safety instruction. The accuracy and limits of the selected simulation need review.
solution:
  title: "Keep the Next Question in View"
  content: "In this lesson idea, the teacher follows the learner's prediction and choices alongside the explanation. Those details help frame the next conversation."
  items:
    - "Agree the learning goal and common assessment criteria. Ask learners what they expect the spring to do and record that starting point."
    - "Introduce the spring task and talk through what changes and what stays fixed. Explain the simulation’s limits before practice."
    - "Change the load, try again and record the extension each time. Compare what happened with the first prediction."
    - "Invite a short explanation or report tied to the learning goal. Ask whether it accounts for the pattern the learner observed."
    - "Offer feedback on the idea, the control of the experiment or both. Revisit the same goal after support before considering a wider activity."
evaluation:
  title: "Look Back With the Teacher and Class"
  content: "Compare evidence with the agreed baseline and review the experience with teachers and learners. The scenario promises no learning improvement, adoption level or time saving."
  items:
    - "Can learners explain why the spring stays fixed, alongside their prediction and recorded extensions, using the agreed criteria?"
    - "What part might classroom conditions, task design and learners' starting points play in any change in performance?"
    - "What do teachers and learners say about the instructions, usability, accessibility and support they need?"
    - "Does the practical record give the teacher enough detail to choose feedback beyond a count of attempts?"

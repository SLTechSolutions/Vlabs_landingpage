salutation: "Dear partners, teachers, and learners,"
paragraphs:
  - "I am writing to thank you for the care you bring to learning. To the institutions and teachers who work with us, and to the learners and teachers who use VLABS: your time and attention matter to me. Behind a lesson is preparation, patience, and the hope that a question will find room to be explored."
  - "When I think about why this work matters, I picture a classroom just before an experiment. A learner sketches a prediction. A classmate asks why the line bends. The teacher waits before answering. I want VLABS to make room for that pause, when curiosity is still taking shape and an explanation has not yet been settled."
  - "For me, the teacher belongs at the centre of this work. A simulation needs a question worth asking and someone who can help learners interpret what they see. Teachers choose the purpose of the activity, explain the limits of the model, and connect the discussion to their lesson. We should respect that judgement, rather than ask a screen to take its place."
  - "I also want to be clear about the place of simulation. It complements practical work carried out with appropriate supervision; it does not replace it. Handling equipment, noticing the texture of a material, and learning to work responsibly beside other people belong to hands-on learning. A model on a screen has limits, and those limits deserve to be part of the conversation."
  - "The questions that interest me extend across physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics, and the wider science classroom. What changes when a condition changes? How does an observation fit a prediction? Where does a model stop being useful? I see these as invitations to think with a teacher, not as reasons to skip the practical work that helps us understand a subject."
  - "To our partners, thank you for bringing the realities of teaching into our conversations: the preparation before a lesson, the shared device, the timetable, and the responsibility of caring for learners. I value the questions that make us stop and reconsider. They remind us that educational technology has to be discussed in the context of an actual classroom, not only on its own terms."
  - "To everyone using VLABS, thank you for giving it your attention. I am interested in the point where a learner hesitates, the instruction a teacher has to explain again, and the question that remains after an activity. You do not need to turn those moments into polished feedback. Telling us plainly what felt unclear, useful, or out of place is a valuable way to begin a conversation."
  - "I hope we can keep listening with that same openness. Feedback from partners, teachers, and learners helps us think about what deserves attention next, while the judgement and care of teachers remain essential. This work deserves care. For me, that means staying close to the questions people bring, and being honest about what a simulation can and cannot offer."

closing: "Thank you for making room for this conversation. If there is a question from your classroom you would like to share, I would be glad to hear it."
